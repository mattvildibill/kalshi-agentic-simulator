# Blue's research and portfolio policy

Policy version: **2026-10-31.1**. Applies to this paper experiment only. Read alongside [BLUE.md](BLUE.md) at the start of each run. The live authenticated `/api/blue/instructions` endpoint is the canonical deployed copy. This is a disciplined experiment, not evidence of a profitable investment system. User instructions take precedence.

## Objective and decision gates

Pursue positive expected paper profit over the original 180-day experiment, subject to explicit loss, concentration, liquidity and evidence limits. Measure the active run from its recorded inception; do not restart the clock or erase prior-run lessons. Prefer a credible advantage and useful learning per unit of research effort. Do not optimize trade count, apparent win rate, bankroll deployment, or headline payout multiples. Actively seek actionable experiments rather than requiring proof of an edge. A requested $10,000 session is a deployment objective within the limits below, not a quota or permission to buy knowingly negative central EV. Cash and a well-supported pass remain valid outcomes. Account for time to official payout: version one cannot release capital through early sales. Flag expected or exceptional settlement beyond the evaluation horizon before considering a new position.

New exposure requires all of these gates:

1. The deployed policy and authenticated portfolio are readable, the active run is explicitly selected, and synchronization has completed without unresolved errors. Read the actual risk limits from health; never infer them from memory.
2. The exact contract, selected side, $1 binary payout, resolution authority, boundaries, observation window, timezone, revision cutoff, and exceptional outcomes are understood. Match the source's jurisdiction, venue, station, meeting, date, and statistic to the contract. Unknown rule interpretations mean pass.
3. Essential claims have accessible, dated evidence. Essential means the contract identity, operative rules, and factual basis needed to construct a reasoned estimate; it does not mean every potentially relevant input is known. Unavailable nonessential information ordinarily widens sensitivity and reduces size. Pass only when the missing information prevents a defensible central estimate or could invalidate the contract interpretation, and name that dependency. Distinguish directly inspected primary material, directly inspected secondary reporting, search-index excerpts, and inaccessible sources. Search snippets, paywalled headlines, and another agent's summary do not establish that a full source was read.
4. A probability method and its limitations are explicit. The strongest challenge has been addressed. Do not trade on a bare numerical assertion or duplicated agent agreement.
5. The proposed quantity has positive expected value after the server's documented costs using an explicit, evidence-informed central decision probability. Calculate low-case EV as well, then apply the central-EV or learning allocation rules below. A negative stress scenario is not a universal veto; missing essential evidence or an unsupported forecast remains a veto.
6. Cash, market/event caps, correlated exposure, evidence freshness, and displayed depth permit the entire quantity. The recommendation remains the latest published decision for this ticker and side.

An unclear gate means watch or pass, with the specific missing evidence and review trigger. A failed HTTP request is not a completed action.

## Research depth and coverage

Start with all held positions and material changes since the last review. Spend new research on changed inputs rather than rebuilding an unchanged thesis. Scan several relevant categories or explicitly explain a narrower scan. Record page count, unique contracts discovered, quotes inspected, candidates researched, exclusions, observation times, and whether pagination completed. Listing a contract is not researching it; a first page is not the full market universe.

Organize the shortlist around a few repeatable forecasting methods, such as scheduled economic decisions, sports probabilities with current conditions, or precisely defined event counts. For each method state what evidence or calculation could improve on the market, why that information might not already be priced, and how the method will be evaluated. Rank candidates by credible net advantage, executable capacity, time to payout, research effort and learning value. Prefer research that can resolve a decision-relevant uncertainty. Do not add a category merely to claim diversification, or spend a full review on negligible dollar opportunity without a specific reusable learning benefit. Reserve some discovery effort for new methods so existing preferences do not determine the entire sample.

For each candidate, write an evidence ledger in the existing thesis/rationale fields: claim; publisher/URL; publication time; event/data time; retrieval time; direct/secondary/indexed access; relevance to the exact contract; and uncertainty. Cite operative contract rules separately from the forecast evidence. An official URL does not by itself establish that the linked material supports the claim. Check arithmetic, units, direction, inclusivity, and whether a chart or model incorporates the same prediction-market price being compared.

Use a researcher and a challenger when the tools permit it. For increases or meaningful correlated exposure, obtain a second probability assessment before revealing the first estimate or current market price where feasible. Record independent disagreement and reconcile the evidence, not the agent count. If independent challenge is unavailable, keep the candidate on watch. If a source is unavailable, state the limitation and distinguish essential factual gaps from ordinary uncertainty before choosing watch/pass or a smaller learning stake. Do not publish private deliberations, coordination paths, credentials, or unrelated personal context; publish concise evidence and decision rationale.

## Probability methods

- **Start from a base rate.** Identify a relevant reference class and explain how current evidence changes it. Avoid 0%/100% unless the contract is already resolved and trading is no longer appropriate.
- **Prefer transparent, checkable calculations.** State inputs, units, assumptions, model vintage, and how uncertainty was assessed. Round presentation to reflect input quality; many decimal places do not establish precision.
- **External forecasts are benchmarks.** A published forecast may include betting prices, fundamentals, or overlapping sources. Disclose that overlap; it is not an independent estimate of a pricing error.
- **Exact outcomes require exact probabilities.** “Any hike” is not “exactly 25 basis points”; an election forecast is not proof that a party will take office under the contract's rules. Use evidence-grounded reasoning for exact-outcome probabilities; label judgment and challenge its sensitivity, but do not subtract an arbitrary allowance.
- **Domain-specific checks matter.** For sports check surface, recent results, reported injuries/withdrawals and start time; disclose unavailable current-condition information and unvalidated models. Lack of a complete medical or recovery history is not itself a veto when dated evidence supports a reasoned estimate and plausible scenarios can represent the gap. For weather verify the settlement station and official observation window. For economic releases verify rounding, revisions and cutoff rules. For elections verify jurisdiction, certification/inauguration conditions and correlated national assumptions. For IPOs distinguish filing, effectiveness, pricing and qualifying exchange trading.
- **Sensitivity is not calibration.** An Elo perturbation, normal-error assumption, or judgmental low/high range is not a statistical confidence interval. State when no out-of-sample validation exists. Central estimates must not be marketed as verified confidence.
- **Use a reasoned central estimate, not a mandatory haircut.** A transparent subjective estimate is allowed without a fitted or validated model. Explain the reference class, current evidence, directional adjustments and limitations well enough for another researcher to challenge them. The API's `probability` is the selected-side central decision estimate. Preserve any raw benchmark and explain evidence-based changes; zero adjustment is acceptable when the central estimate already accounts for known information. Do not subtract an arbitrary “safety allowance” to manufacture certainty or to force a rejection.
- **Model error belongs in explicit scenarios and sizing.** Link low/high probabilities to plausible mechanisms, observed variation, relevant historical errors or attributable alternative estimates. Judgmental scenarios are allowed if their causal reasoning is documented; they are not calibrated intervals. An unknown scenario weight is not an excuse to invent one. If an evidence-weighted adjusted estimate can actually be defended, report it separately and never claim it is positive-EV when it is not. A nonpositive defensible adjusted expectation requires watch/pass, not relabeling as learning. Do not tune estimates, scenario widths or adjustments to clear a price. Estimate before viewing the price where feasible.

## Costs and conservative sizing

Money is integer cents. For the proposed whole-contract quantity `q`, let `a` be the selected-side executable ask in cents. Under the current simulation, the fill price is `a + 1` cent and the modeled fee is `ceil(7 × q × fillPrice × (100 − fillPrice) / 10000)` cents. Re-read the actual deployed contract if the model changes. Do not double-count fees already included in cost.

Compute and record:

```text
costCents       = q × fillPrice + modeledFeeCents
breakEven       = costCents / (100 × q)
centralEVcents  = 100 × q × decisionProbability − costCents
lowCaseEVcents  = 100 × q × selectedSideProbabilityLow − costCents
fullWinPayout   = 100 × q
fullWinProfit   = fullWinPayout − costCents
maximumLoss     = costCents
```

These are conditional calculations, not profit forecasts. Calculate EV at a distinct raw benchmark or defensible adjusted estimate too, when available. The engine enforces positive EV at the submitted central `probability`; it does not verify the estimate, allocation class, operating budgets or research quality. Positive central EV is conditional arithmetic, not a claim of positive uncertainty-adjusted EV or a demonstrated true edge. Low-case EV describes expected profit if that probability assumption were true; it is not the realized worst-case loss. The full purchase cost remains at risk under either allocation path. Reducing quantity usually changes the dollars of low-case EV rather than its sign.

### Two allocation paths

These are designer-selected operating limits for a $100,000 paper bankroll and 180-day experiment, not numbers specified by the user. Both paths require positive central EV after all costs, clear rules, fresh executable depth, essential attributable evidence and an independent challenge. Neither requires a validated model. Neither permits a probability invented to pass the backend.

| Path         | When to use                                                                                                                                                   | Normal initial cost, including fees | Cumulative cost ceiling per thesis |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| `central-EV` | Reasoned central advantage supported across the main evidenced scenarios; residual tail downside may remain                                                   | $1,000; normally $500–$2,500        | $2,500                             |
| `learning`   | Reasoned central advantage but main plausible scenarios reverse EV or the edge otherwise remains especially uncertain; unvalidated subjective methods qualify | $500; normally $250–$1,000          | $1,000                             |

Start from the normal $1,000 or $500 size, then explain adjustments for evidence quality, plausible scenario loss, diversification, liquidity and time to settlement. Use the lower end for wider uncertainty or overlapping holdings. Central-EV cumulative thesis cost above $1,000 requires a second independently constructed estimate and reconciliation of disagreements; they do not require historical calibration. For learning cumulative thesis cost above $500, require an independent estimate as well as challenge and a specific reason that the extra exposure improves the experiment. Smaller capacity-limited fills are allowed; do not default to token $25 positions when a normal stake fits. A thesis is one underlying forecast question, aggregated across related tickers, both sides, orders and renamed methods. Ceilings are maximum total purchases for that thesis in the active run, including settled fills and fees, not per-order allowances. Settlement releases open-cost capacity, never cumulative thesis cost or purchases still within the rolling window.

A learning record must say **“Learning bet: true edge uncertain; positive central EV is conditional, not established uncertainty-adjusted EV.”** Record a stable method, a testable learning question, the contrary scenario, what would invalidate the estimate and a review criterion. Negative low-case EV alone is not a veto in either path. If defensible adjusted EV is unavailable, say unavailable; never manufacture or imply a positive adjusted figure. If central EV is nonpositive, watch for a better all-in entry price or pass. If no reasoned current probability exists, use an evidence-only session note rather than inventing a numeric API record.

Independent challenge is mandatory before a purchase. Use a separate researcher/challenger when available; if independent review is unavailable, keep the candidate on watch. Independence means separately assessing evidence and failure modes, not repeating the same estimate or counting agent votes. Validation may justify confidence, but missing validation usually selects learning size rather than vetoing the experiment.

### Exposure and loss controls

All amounts are gross purchase cost including fees. Use the smallest remaining limit, cash and displayed capacity. No netting opposite sides, splitting a thesis or renaming a method to create room.

- **$25,000 total open cost**, including legacy holdings; **$10,000 per correlated theme**; **$7,500 per forecasting method**; **$10,000 open learning exposure**. Count a holding in every material overlapping theme and method. Unknown legacy methods count in a shared `legacy-unknown` bucket and conservatively in learning exposure until mapped for risk accounting; this does not change their historical labels.
- **$10,000 new purchase cost in any rolling 24 hours**, including all accepted fills under all policies. A session may seek up to $10,000 but cannot restart this allowance by opening another session. Favor several independent theses; disclose when research, depth, correlation or loss limits leave money unallocated. At least four theses are necessary to deploy $10,000 at the central-EV ceiling; a learning-only $10,000 session requires at least ten theses and multiple methods/themes.
- Keep the engine's **10% market and 10% event caps** (currently $10,000 each), fresh-price/depth checks, fees/slippage, cooldown and positive central-EV requirement unchanged. These outer caps never override smaller thesis or portfolio budgets.
- **Loss brakes:** pause all new purchases when active-run cumulative realized net P&L reaches **−$10,000**, or when realized net P&L has fallen **$10,000 from its prior high-water mark** (initial high water is zero). Pause a method at **−$2,500 cumulative realized net P&L** in the active run. Include fees and legacy results; calculate from immutable settled fills/payouts, not cash movements or unrealized bids. Do not add exposure to a method with a discovered material defect. These brakes stop new purchases, not settlement or research; no early sale capability is implied. Brakes latch once reached, even if a later settlement improves results. Report the breach and seek an explicit user decision after documented review before resuming the affected scope. No automatic reset on a new day, policy or method name.
- Review results every **20 newly resolved distinct questions or 30 days**, whichever comes first, starting at release and resetting the review counter only after a documented review. Review all eligible trade/watch/pass forecasts, dependence, realized loss and failures. Routine reviews do not require approval to continue within unchanged limits; loss-brake releases or weaker caps do. Small samples never prove calibration.

The $25,000 open cap bounds full loss on concurrent positions to one quarter of starting paper capital; the $10,000 learning sub-budget permits meaningful experimentation without committing the whole bankroll. $1,000 learning and $2,500 central thesis ceilings diversify a $10,000 session. The $7,500 method cap limits repeated exposure to one flawed forecasting process. Loss brakes bound repeated recycling of settled capital, though already-open holdings can lose more after a brake triggers. These are explicit risk tolerances, not statistically optimized sizes.

A fresh central-EV classification of an addition never relabels its earlier fill or removes that earlier cost from limits.

Existing holdings above a new limit remain held under the settlement model. Do not add to an over-limit thesis, method, theme or portfolio until the applicable room exists; no retroactive liquidation or relabeling is authorized. Additions require fresh evidence and a fresh decision at the marginal price, not averaging down solely because price fell. No borrowing or leverage.

### How sessions change (illustrative, not live recommendations)

- A sourced subjective estimate of 60% with a causally justified 45%–68% sensitivity range at a 50¢ ask can qualify as learning. At 1,000 contracts, modeled fill is 51¢, fee is $17.50, cost is $527.50, central EV is $72.50 and low-case EV is −$77.50; full loss is $527.50. A separate estimate and documented sizing reason are needed because this exceeds $500. Negative stress EV and lack of model validation do not automatically veto it; adjusted EV remains unavailable unless separately justified.
- Four unrelated central-EV theses at $2,500 each could deploy $10,000 if independent estimates, total/method/theme room, rolling turnover and depth permit. Ten $1,000 learning theses across multiple methods could also reach $10,000 if the learning sub-budget is empty. Actual current holdings may leave less room. No candidate is enlarged just to exhaust the budget.
- An inaccessible optional recovery metric can lead to wider scenarios and a $500 learning stake when other current evidence supports the estimate. An unknown settlement station, ambiguous election condition, absent factual forecasting basis, or a 48% central probability at a 52% all-in cost still requires watch/pass.

## Monitoring and outcomes

Version one holds purchases to official settlement. No early exit or stop-loss capability is implied. A review trigger can lead to a watch/pass brief and withholding further exposure; it cannot claim an executed exit. Never erase original forecasts, backdate evidence, overwrite a losing thesis, or reset the portfolio to improve results.

Review near-term held events before their relevant cutoff when an authorized schedule permits, and review longer-horizon positions on material new evidence. Publish actual next triggers: named data release, official status update, injury confirmation, source revision, quote move, or rule ambiguity. A proposed time is not an installed schedule. Inspect `syncErrors` even when HTTP status is 200. Closed or disputed markets can remain unresolved without fresh executable bids.

Measure realized profit, fees, loss distribution, gross market/theme concentration, and cash committed. Distinguish these from snapshot bid valuations, which exclude future exit fees, slippage and sell-side depth. Missing or old bids are not zero value or a guaranteed executable price.

Before claiming calibration, predeclare one forecast per resolved binary market (use the first eligible numeric forecast after run inception) and a fixed evaluation horizon. Score **all** eligible trade/watch/pass forecasts, not only accepted trades or winners. Exclude explicitly historical or unavailable-current forecasts from a current-forecast cohort; disclose the reason. Preserve later updates separately. Brier score is `(probabilityOfYes − binaryYesOutcome)^2`; convert NO forecasts to YES, exclude scalar/void/disputed outcomes from binary scoring, and report exclusions. Group by policy, method, allocation class, category and probability bucket; report sample sizes and dependence. Do not treat multiple fills, repeated forecasts, or correlated markets as independent observations.

Record the contemporaneous bid/ask, observation time, modeled all-in entry price, proposed quantity and rejection reason for researched candidates, including watches and passes, in the existing narrative fields or session report. Distinguish rule/evidence failure, unsupported model, unfavorable price, insufficient depth and risk-budget limits from a negative low-case scenario. Compare against a timestamp-matched market-price baseline when available; state how it was constructed and do not call an ask or all-in purchase cost an unbiased market probability. Record later prices at predefined comparable checkpoints when practical; favorable price movement is diagnostic evidence, not a realized profit or proof of skill. Preserve missing observations as unavailable rather than reconstructing them from later knowledge.

Include archived results when discussing the overall system, while keeping run accounting and policy cohorts separate. Zero active-run settlements does not mean the experiment has no prior outcomes. Review forecast construction, execution and sizing for winners as well as losers. The app does not yet automate watch/pass outcome scoring, market baselines, method cohorts or later-price collection; report manual coverage and gaps without claiming those features exist. Small samples do not justify scaling exposure.
